# Short examples

These are illustrations only. **Their numbers and segments are not
defaults.** Every real run uses its own evidence.

## A provisional memo from one paragraph (excerpt)

> **Idea:** a mobile bike-repair van visiting office parks weekly.
> **Research paths:** public research did not run (no web tools);
> structured access not configured; direct evidence: none supplied.

| Field | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Customer problem | Commuters with a broken bike lose a commute day, *untested assumption*. No incidents supplied (strength: None, no behavior observed). |
| Market opportunity | `parks reachable × commuters who cycle per park × repairs per year × price`. All inputs unknown. Source unavailable: verify via the employers' commute surveys and a city bike-count dataset. |
| Claude recommendation | **Test further**: no evidence yet of frequency or willingness to pay. |
| Next test | T1: a two-week sign-up and pay-on-service trial at one park. |

## A question batch

> 1. I understand the van would serve office parks, and that employers
>    might pay instead of riders. **Who would sign the contract: facilities,
>    HR, or each rider?** This decides whether the market count is in
>    employers or in riders, and who the pilot must recruit.
> 2. **Walk me through the last time someone at a target park had a bike
>    problem at work. What did they do?** This tells us whether the problem
>    is frequent enough to test first.

## A test card

| Field | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Assumption | Riders at one park will book and pay for on-site repairs. |
| Method | A two-week pay-on-service pilot, booked through one sign-up page. |
| Sample | Riders at Park A, reached through the facilities newsletter. |
| Measure and denominator | Paid bookings out of the unique riders who opened the newsletter. |
| Success threshold | ≥ 3% of openers book and pay (*proposed, team to confirm*). |
| Limits | ≤ 2 site days, ≤ $400 in parts, 2 weeks. |
| Next decision | met → a second park with an employer-paid offer; missed → interview non-bookers before reshaping. |

## A scenario: "What if the employer pays instead of the rider?"

| Item | Baseline (handoff v1) | Scenario | Change |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Buyer | rider | employer (facilities) | buyer changed |
| Market unit | riders | parks under contract | the count must be redone |
| Price | per repair | monthly per park | the pricing test must be redone |

- **Still valid:** problem evidence from riders (E2, E3).
- **Needs retesting:** willingness to pay (E5 measured riders), and the
  channel (the newsletter reaches riders, not budget holders).
- **Recommendation:** still Test further, but the test changes to employer
  interviews.
- **Approval scope:** the approved pilot was rider-paid, so the change goes
  back to the team for review.

## An evidence change

> **v2 delta:** the supplied pilot log (E9) shows 11 of 240 openers booked
> and paid (4.6%), above the proposed 3% threshold. The findings on the
> customer problem and on feasibility are unchanged. The market forecast is
> updated with the observed conversion for this park only. Recommendation:
> Test further → Advance, to a second park. Approval scope: the approved
> pilot is unchanged, and the next commitment needs a new team decision.
